## What counts each week

Every Wednesday tutorial in weeks 1–12 is assessed. There are **two points per week**:

**Attendance · 1 point.** Attend the scheduled tutorial and check in with the tutor,
who records your attendance for that week.

**Lab participation · 1 point.** Make a prediction, try the week's experiment or compare
two cases, then explain what changed and why. The tutor checks your work and explanation
during the session.

Show the tutor your run, trace, table or notes before leaving. A spoken explanation or
a short written explanation is equally acceptable. You may discuss experiments with a
partner, but each student checks in and explains their own reasoning.

You do not need a perfect algorithm or a successful delivery to earn the participation
point. A failed experiment counts when you can explain the failure and what you would
test next. Simply opening the page or copying a result without explaining it is not
participation. There is no mark for collecting screenshots or for visual polish.

## How the total is calculated

All twelve weeks count equally; there are no dropped weeks. The maximum is **24 raw
points**, converted to **20 percentage points** of the course grade:

**Tutorial contribution = (points earned ÷ 24) × 20.**

For example, 18 out of 24 earns 15 percentage points towards the course total. The
attendance half and participation half each contribute up to 10 percentage points.
The rubric below splits this tutorial component; its percentages are not additional
course weight.

## If you miss a session

Contact the tutor as soon as you can. For an approved absence or access need, agree an
equivalent activity and a check-in with the teaching team. The team will confirm how
attendance and participation credit apply. Unarranged absences receive no attendance
credit; opening the Lab later does not automatically record either point.

## How this connects to the assignments

Keep your weekly evidence: it helps you build Assignment 1 (30%) and Assignment 2 (50%).
Tutorial points reward attendance and engagement; assignment marks assess the submitted
implementation, validation and analysis. The same experiment can inform both, but the
assignment still needs your own code and explanation.

The tutor keeps the weekly record. This demonstration website does not record attendance,
award points or receive submissions.
