# Policies and boundaries

## 1. What the simulation is

Slop Hill, the kitchen, the twenty orders, the two drone types, the two charging pads and
the corridor are a teaching model. The constraints come from a real task; the numbers,
the safety margins and the rules are course settings, chosen so that each week's question
has a clean answer. Nothing on this site claims that a real drone, a real airspace rule or
a real delivery business works this way. A result here is a result about this model.

Every page says which model it assumes. The model grows in five steps — weeks 1–3, week 4,
weeks 5–6, weeks 7–8, weeks 9–12 — and a plan that was correct under an earlier step is
not wrong in hindsight when a later step changes the answer. Results under different steps
are labelled and never compared as if they were the same conditions.

## 2. Parameters

The canonical data is five files: the map, the orders, the fleet, the pads and the rules.
Their checksums are pinned in the repository's own tests, and every weekly case is a
subset or a view of them. The table below lists what the model depends on and which values
are fixed. Values marked *to be set* are calibrated against a reference run before the data
is released, and published here with their range and source when they are.

| Parameter | Value or range | Status |
|---|---|---|
| Area | About 2 km × 2 km; about 120 m from the kitchen to the hilltop | fixed |
| Depot | The kitchen at the foot of the hill; every task starts and ends there | fixed |
| Orders | #01–#20; ready time, weight, promised time | to be set; the roles of #07, #13 and #20 are fixed |
| Drone types | L and H: speed, payload, battery | to be set; #20 exceeds L's payload |
| Energy | Level flight per metre; climb per metre of rise; descent per metre of drop; hover per tick; loading and service | to be set; uphill always costs more than downhill of the same length |
| Charging pads | Two; effective rate; turnaround time | two fixed; rate and turnaround to be set |
| Time | Integer ticks; tick length published with each scenario | to be set |
| Evening | 18:00–21:00 ready and delivery; one evaluation cut-off | to be set |
| Objective | Total lateness, then all-returned time, then total energy, in that order | fixed |
| Design target | A complete feasible baseline exists for the normal batch; the reference method delivers at least N orders on time | N to be set |

If the data does not meet the design target, the data changes, not the promise.

## 3. What a static site can and cannot do

This site is static. Everything it computes runs in your browser, and everything it holds
— the data files, the reference plans, the cases — can be read by anyone who opens the
page source. The reference plans on the site are labelled *reference plan, re-validated
live*: the plan was computed in advance; the validator checks it again in front of you.

The workbench's plan check is a local pre-check. It runs the same validator the course
uses, but it is not a submission and not a mark. Nothing on this site says "received" or
"submitted", because no service behind it could make that true. Formal submission and
marking happen through the assignment pages' stated process, on the fixed cases.

Your own records stay in your browser unless you export them. Export before you clear
your browser data; the site cannot recover them.

## 4. Assignments

Both assignments are individual work. Discussing the problems with other students is
expected; submitting the same code or the same analysis is not. Name every person you
discussed the work with and every external source, including code, in the analysis.

AI assistance is allowed and must be declared. It does not replace your own explanation:
the analysis is yours, in your words, and you should be able to defend any line of it in
a conversation. A generated report is not an experiment; a claim about a result points at
your code, your input and your run record.

Every claim of feasibility is checked by the validator before performance is compared. A
plan with a constraint violation is not a valid plan. An accurate diagnosis of why a plan
fails earns analysis marks; a plan that "works" with a dropped order or a changed parameter
earns none.

Late work loses 5% of the assignment's marks per calendar day, for up to five days; after
that it is not marked. Ask the convenor for an extension before the due date, with the
reason; extensions after the due date are given only for documented circumstances.

## 5. Accessibility

Every pointer action has a keyboard path: ordering has move-up and move-down buttons,
selection has lists, playback has previous and next event buttons, and the 3D views have
orbit buttons. Every 3D view has a text description and a result table beside it, and
nothing on the site is conveyed by colour alone. The site respects your reduced-motion
setting: nothing autoplays, and every animation has a static result and a step button.
The core operations — change, run, compare, locate, export — work at desktop width and on
a phone; the phone shows tables first and loads 3D only when you ask for it.

If something is not reachable for you, tell the tutor; that is a bug in the site, not a
limit you should work around.

## 6. Who to ask

The tutor first, for anything in the workbench, your code, a counterexample that will not
behave, or the practice-pack format — in the Wednesday tutorial, or by email when it
cannot wait. The convenor for what the course expects, why a rule is the way it is,
marking and extensions. Both are on the people page.
