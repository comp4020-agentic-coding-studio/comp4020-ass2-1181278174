## Each route is legal. Together they are not.

| Drone | Wants the corridor | From the plan |
|---|---|---|
| B | `[0, 6)` | entered from G at 0, out at 6 |
| A | `[3, 5)` | at P at 3, across by 5 |

Overlap test: `max(0, 3) = 3` is less than `min(6, 5) = 5`. They are in the corridor at the
same time. Neither plan is wrong on its own.

---

## What a conflict is

- A **resource** with an id: this corridor, both directions.
- Each use is a **half-open interval** `[start, end)`.
- Two uses conflict when `max(s1, s2)` is less than `min(e1, e2)`.

Not "did they collide in this frame". The whole interval, or nothing.

```notes
Half-open matters: B leaving at 6 and A entering at 6 do not overlap. Any safety buffer the
course wants is added to the intervals first, then the same test runs.
```

---

## A path is places. A trajectory is places with times.

Week 2 searched over `place`. That answered "which way".

This week the state is `(place, time)`. `(P, 3)` and `(P, 4)` are two different states,
because the future is different from each of them.

---

## The bug that deletes waiting

An implementation that remembers `visited[P]` refuses `(P, 4)`, `(P, 5)`, `(P, 6)` — it has
"been to P". It reports **no path**.

With time in the state the legal sequence is:

`(P, 3) → (P, 4) → (P, 5) → (P, 6) → (G, 8)`

Wait three ticks, cross in two.

---

## Waiting is not free

- Waiting is allowed only at **waiting points**. P is one.
- On the ground it costs time. In the air it costs time **and energy**, and it occupies the
  waiting point.
- A at P is in the air. Three ticks of hover come off the same budget week 4 checked.

Time in the state does not solve energy. The engine keeps both.

---

## The reservation table

| Resource | Drone | Interval | Result |
|---|---|---|---|
| corridor | B | `[0, 6)` | held |
| corridor | A | `[3, 5)` | refused: overlaps B |
| corridor | A | `[6, 8)` | granted |

A route is a proposal. A reservation is a commitment. The table is what the second drone
plans against.

---

## Three ways to arrange it

| Arrangement | Corridor | A's delivery | Cost |
|---|---|---|---|
| both depart as planned | conflict `[3, 5)` | — | not a plan |
| B first, A waits at P | A `[6, 8)` | 3 ticks later | 3 ticks of hover |
| A detours around the block | none | 5 ticks later | longer flight |

The first one is the one the two shortest paths give you.

---

## Which one is right?

Week 5 fixed the objective: total lateness first, then the time every drone is back, then
energy. Under that objective, waiting beats the detour here.

Change the promised time for #13, or add a third drone that also wants the corridor, and
the answer changes. That is why the choice is computed, not assumed.

## Next week: search in time

- A* again, over `(place, phase, time)`, under the reservations already committed.
- Drones planned one at a time, in a priority order; each commits a full round trip.
- What it guarantees: the best route **given** the others. What it does not: the best plan
  for all of them together.

```notes
This is Cooperative A* in Silver's sense: the space-time search per drone, the reservation
table shared. Priority order matters and the tutorial in week 10 swaps it.
```
